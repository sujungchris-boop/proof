Conference & Summit Venues in Seoul for Web3 (COEX and Beyond)

By PROOF · Seoul · June 2026 · 7 min read

A conference is the deep end of event production — hundreds or thousands of guests, multiple tracks, sponsors, and no room for improvisation. Choosing the venue is the first move, and it sets the ceiling on everything else. Here's how we approach large-format spaces in Seoul.

If a dinner is a scalpel and a party is a spotlight, a conference is a machine. Everything scales at once — capacity, production, logistics, risk — and the venue is the chassis you build it all on. Get the room right and the event has room to breathe; get it wrong and no amount of programming saves it. This is the large-format companion to our Seoul venue guide.

COEX: the default for scale

For conference-scale events, Seoul's anchor is COEX in Gangnam — the convention complex that hosts the city's major conferences and exhibitions, and where we've produced Seoul Meta Week. It offers the auditoriums, exhibition halls and infrastructure a multi-track program needs, plus the central location and hotel density that make a large guest list feasible. During KBW, much of the ecosystem already orbits this area — a real advantage for foot traffic and convenience.

Beyond COEX

  • Large hotel convention floors — premium venues like The Shilla Seoul carry mid-size summits with hospitality and service built in.
  • Dedicated convention & exhibition halls — purpose-built spaces for exhibitions and trade-show-style layouts.
  • Auditoriums & theaters — ideal for a single-track, stage-forward summit where the keynote is the centerpiece.
  • University & institutional halls — a credible, cost-effective option for developer-focused or academic-leaning programs.
Match the format: multi-track + expo → convention complex · mid-size summit → hotel convention floor · keynote-led → auditorium · developer/hacker → institutional hall.

Capacity is a layout question, not a headline number

A venue's advertised capacity is a starting point, not an answer. The same hall seats very differently for a theater-style keynote, a banquet, or an expo floor with booths and lounges. Plan backwards from your program — main stage, breakouts, sponsor space, registration, catering, networking — and confirm the room supports that layout, not just the raw number. Overshooting the space drains energy as badly as undershooting drains comfort.

The venue is only the shell

At conference scale, the room is maybe a third of the job. What actually decides the day: multi-track AV and staging, sponsor and exhibition fulfilment, registration and crowd flow, catering for hundreds, connectivity that holds under load, and a run-of-show that keeps it all on time. A great hall with weak production is a common, expensive failure. This is where an experienced operator earns their place — the difference between a venue booked and an event delivered.

Lead time: the longest of any format

Conference venues demand the most runway. Large halls book months to a year out, and the production planning behind a multi-track event needs every bit of that time. Around KBW the squeeze is worse. If a summit is on your roadmap, the venue conversation should start now — the booking principles in booking a Seoul venue for KBW apply, at a longer horizon. And if a full conference is more than you need, a sharper side event or activation may hit your goal for a fraction of the lift.

Seoul is built to host Web3 at conference scale — COEX and its peers give you the room. The win is in matching the hall to your program, backing it with production that holds, and starting early enough to do both well.
