KBW vs TOKEN2049: Where Should Your Web3 Project Show Up?

By PROOF · Seoul · September 2026 · 6 min read

Two weeks dominate the Web3 event calendar, and most teams can't do both at full strength. The right pick isn't the bigger name — it's the one that matches what you need the trip to deliver. Here's how we'd decide.

We run side events in Seoul during Korea Blockchain Week and support teams around TOKEN2049. This is our operator's view of the choice — not a ranking. If you'd rather start from formats, see choosing the right Web3 activation.

The short version

  • KBW is a city-scale week: dense, relationship-driven, and the natural gateway to Korean exchanges, investors, communities and media.
  • TOKEN2049 is a global cross-section: the broadest international audience of founders, funds and vendors in one place.

Choose KBW if…

  • Korea is a target market, or you're weighing an entry. Start with our Korea market-entry playbook.
  • You want depth over breadth: fewer, better conversations with people who can actually act.
  • You want a memorable format — a dinner, a venue with a story — rather than a booth among hundreds. See Seoul Web3 event venues.

Choose TOKEN2049 if…

  • Your priority is global awareness and meeting international investors and partners at scale.
  • Your audience is spread across regions and you need one place to reach many of them.
  • You're ready to compete for attention in a crowded side-event schedule. Our TOKEN2049 side-event checklist covers what that takes.

How the two differ in practice

  • Noise level. Both are crowded. In a single-city week, a well-run event stands out through venue and guest curation; in a global week, discoverability is the harder problem.
  • Access. In both, the best venues and vendors are held by operators with standing relationships. Booking cold and late is the common failure — see booking a Seoul venue for KBW.
  • Follow-up. A smaller, curated room makes personal follow-up realistic. A very large one needs a capture system decided before the event.
Rule of thumb: pick by goal. Korea entry and high-trust relationships point to KBW; broad global reach points to TOKEN2049. If you do both, make one the flagship and keep the other deliberately lighter.

If you can only pick one

Ask what result would make the trip a success: five serious Korean conversations, or a wide net across regions? Write that down before comparing budgets. Then pick the week where that result is most likely, and choose the format — booth, side event, dinner — that fits it.

Either week rewards early decisions on format and venue. Decide the goal first, and the rest of the plan gets much easier.

FAQ

Should I do KBW or TOKEN2049?

It depends on the goal. Choose KBW if Korea is a target market or you want a dense, high-access week in one city. Choose TOKEN2049 if you want the widest global audience of investors and founders. Many teams do both, with different formats in each.

Can a project do both KBW and TOKEN2049?

Yes, and many do. They sit at different points in the calendar, so the practical question is format and budget: a lighter presence at one and a flagship at the other usually beats two half-committed efforts.

Which is better for entering the Korean market?

KBW. It concentrates Korean exchanges, investors, communities and media in one week, so a side event there reaches the people a Korea entry actually needs. See our Korea market-entry playbook.

Do I need a local agency for either?

Not strictly, but venue access, vendor relationships and local contracts are where foreign teams lose time. Both weeks reward operators who hold those relationships year-round.
